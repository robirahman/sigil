# Round 3: the guest-game release gate and the browser's real search depth (2026-10-07)

Branch `r3-gate`, from `engine-improvement` (cd38f524). Inputs: `engine/reports/2026-10-guest-audit.md`
and its suite `ai/data/benchmarks/guest_2026-10_cases.json`. One Spot c3d-highcpu-16 in us-east4-a,
run 20261007T174452Z (`gs://focus-surfer-494820-g0-sigil/runs/20261007T174452Z/live/`).

**Summary.**
* The guest suite is now a release gate: `bench_suites.py --guest-only --config "cand:eval=shipped"`.
  Every position in it is a competitive-variant position.
* The gate's main metric does not depend on the eval: of the 10 human blows that left the AI in a forced
  loss, how many does the candidate's search report as a forced loss?
* v27 baseline: 1 of 10 at depth 4, 5 of 10 at depth 5, 6 of 10 at depth 6.
* **The browser's Hard tier reaches only depth 4 to 5 exactly where it matters.** Over a whole game it
  completes depth 6 at its median move (10 s, persistent table). Before the 10 losing blows it reaches
  d6 once (1 of 10) and d5 in 4 of 10. At the 10 avoidable own blunders it reaches d5 once and never d6.
* These are the slowest positions to search: about 27-30k nodes/s, against a median of 64k over the
  game. They are wide cast and dash+cast positions.
* A 2x faster machine moves the decisive positions by about half a ply at most. Ponder adds less.

## 1. The gate

### Suites (all competitive variant)

| file | cases | what a case asks |
|---|---|---|
| `guest_2026-10_cases.json` (existing) | 32 opponent replies; 10 of them are **MATE blows** (target -20: the reply left the AI in a forced loss; all from AI losses) | `sees`: the search of the position before the reply (`sfn`, human to move) values it, from the AI's side, at or below `target` + 0.5. For a MATE case that means the search reports the forced loss. |
| `guest_2026-10_own.json` (**new**, 10) | the AI's own avoidable blunders from near-level positions | `avoids`: the move the search picks at `sfn` (the AI's decision) leads to a different position than the move it played |

`guest_2026-10_own.json` is built with `bench_build.py guest-own` from the three audit runs' own-turn
rows. A row is kept when its run confirmed the fall and its depth-4 value before the move was
>= -1.0. Its depth-6 alternative must also beat the played move by >= 0.25 stones, or depth 6 must
play the same move (a blind case, kept).
* It contains the audit's six "avoidable" blunders: 08:43 t40, 04:45 t22 and t28, 05:07 t19,
  06:13 t22 and t24.
* It adds four more that only v27's audit confirmed: 00:04 t25 and t27, 08:43 t30, 04:45 t26.
* One candidate was dropped: 00:40 t12, where depth 6 rates the alternative worse than the move
  played.

### How to run (machine-independent, about 25 min on 16 vCPUs; the d6 column takes about half of it)

```
python engine/harness/bench_suites.py --guest-only --nodes 50000,300000,600000,1500000 \
    --guest-depths 4,5,6 --config "cand:eval=shipped" --json guest_gate_cand.json
```

* `eval=shipped` (new) runs the loaded build exactly as it ships: its own `SHIPPED_EVAL` and
  `SHIPPED_POLICY`. These are the `SIGIL_AUDIT_ENGINE=shipped` semantics; builds before v24/v25 fall
  back to `tfit` and no policy.
* Add `module=<path.so>` to score another build.
* Budgets are node counts or fixed depths, never clocks.
* The output keeps every case, so a candidate is compared with `ai/data/benchmarks/guest_gate_v27.json`
  case by case.
* The policy-stream coverage of the 32 replies (`rank_of_result` in the stream the search expands) is
  reported too.
* `engine/gcp/runner_r3gate.sh` runs the gate, plus the depth probe below, on one VM.

**Why depths 4, 5 and 6, and 600k nodes.** Section 2 shows the reasons:
* The browser reaches d4 (sometimes d5) at the decisive positions.
* It reaches d6 at its median position in a game.
* It spends a median of 634k nodes per move on the probe machine.

### Pass rule for a release (competitive is the release target)

A candidate replaces v27 only if all of the following hold on this suite:
1. **MATE, browser-realistic:** depth 4 >= 1, depth 5 >= 5 and 600k >= 2 (v27's counts). Its MATE
   set at depth 5 loses no v27 case unless it gains another one.
2. **Own blunders:** avoids at depth 4 and at 600k >= 9.
3. **No competitive regression on the frozen suites.** `bench_suites.py` now reports the sees-rate of
   `surprise_cases_v25.json` on its competitive subset too (299 of 317). That rate must not fall by
   more than 1 percentage point at 300k or 1.5M nodes.
4. **Report, do not gate:** depth 6 and 1.5M MATE counts (improvement targets: v25 reaches 8 at d6),
   the `opp` column, and policy-stream coverage. Give the case-by-case diff against
   `guest_gate_v27.json`, listing every case gained or lost.

Searches are deterministic at fixed budgets. A changed count therefore means the engine changed, not
noise. A 1-case move on 10 cases is still small evidence, so state the cases, not only the totals.

**Where competitive positions are missing elsewhere.** The guest gate is 100% competitive. Elsewhere:

| suite | competitive share |
|---|---|
| surprise | 299/317 |
| human finds | 118/120 |
| final-blow misses | 17/26 |
| `engine/harness/positions_midgame.txt` (the node-rate, parity and wasm-calibration file) | **0/76** |
| `positions_prof12.txt` | **0/12** |
| `positions_expansion.txt` | 4/25 |
| `positions_rust_games.txt` | 58/60 |

**The arena** (`launch.sh` / `runner.sh` / `ab_search.py`) plays the **standard** variant unless
`SIGIL_VARIANT=competitive` is set. An Elo SPRT therefore says nothing about competitive play unless it
runs a competitive arm.

Competitive positions are also the slow ones: 41-51k nodes/s in the 30 s probes against 64k for the
few standard ones. So node-rate work measured on `positions_midgame.txt` overstates the gain where it
matters. Recommendations:
* Re-run node-rate work on `positions_rust_games.txt` or the guest positions.
* Give every release SPRT a competitive arm.

## 2. Browser Hard-tier depth at 10 s

**The tier as shipped:**
* Source: `game-board-local.js` `rust_hard`; `rust-ai.js`; `rust-worker.js`.
* Settings: 10 s per move, `ttBits` 20, `widthScale` 4, eval `nnue_spell3`, adaptive [0.10, 2, 6].
* One worker: option A multicore is off.
* The policy is on through the wasm's `ensure_shipped_policy`, and the opening book is off.
* `exact_clock` spends the whole budget.
* Pondering is on by default for 10 s tiers: 250 ms slices up to depth 12. The table persists through
  the game.

**Probe.** `tools/browser-depth.js` drives `docs/static/wasm` (v27) the way the worker does, with
the per-depth callback, in node 18. It has two modes:
* `games`: replays all 24 guest games with the AI's real history and a persistent table, 10 s per AI
  move, without and with 10 s of ponder per human move.
* `cases`: fresh table, 30 s, every depth's completion time recorded.

8 processes ran on the 8 physical cores of a c3d-highcpu-16, nothing else running. Because the search
is single threaded and `exact_clock` cuts only on wall time, a machine x times as fast completes in
10 s what the probe completed in 10 s * x. So the 30 s probes give the 10 s depth for x <= 3.

**Calibration.** Fixed-depth `bench_hash` / `bench` on `positions_midgame.txt` searches identical trees
natively and in wasm (parity HASH 55a10a3b6c3b7ef9 at d4). Same tree, wall time:

| machine | native | node 18 wasm | headless Chrome 153 wasm |
|---|---|---|---|
| probe VM (c3d, AMD Genoa) | 42 knps | 26 knps | - |
| this Chromebook (i3-1215U, other jobs running) | 43 knps | 29-31 knps | 32-35 knps |

* Chrome's wasm is 1.10-1.14x node's on the same machine (`tools/browser/wasm-speed.js`).
* So x = 1 ≈ Chrome on a Chromebook-class i3 laptop (about 0.9x of it).
* **Assumption:** a typical desktop Chrome is x = 1.5 to 2, and an older laptop or phone is x = 0.5
  to 0.75. These numbers come from single-thread class, not a measurement.
* The v25 report's 85 knps in headless Chromium was 2 s searches on standard midgame positions with
  the lighter `nnue_spell`. It is not comparable with the competitive v27 numbers here.

**Whole games** (339 AI moves of 24 games, all competitive, x = 1, persistent table):

| positions | x | >= d4 | >= d5 | >= d6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| surprise_cases_v25 sample (80; 76 competitive), AI decision | 0.75 / 1 / 1.5 / 2 | 96% | 76 / 80 / 84 / 85% | 45 / 49 / 54 / 57% |

**Answer.** In the audit's decisive positions the browser reaches **d5 in about 4 of 10 and d6 in 1 of
10** before the losing blows. At the own blunders it reaches **d5 in 1-3 of 10 and d6 in none**. A
desktop twice as fast changes this to about 5 of 10 at d5 for the blows and 4 of 10 at the own
blunders, and d6 stays at 1 and 0 of 10.

The audit's "seen at d5/d6" means a search of the reply position (human to move). The AI's own root
search needs one more ply to have the same view. So the d5/d6 fixes the audit found are mostly
beyond the browser:
* The MATE blows need a d6-d7 root. The browser gets one in 1 of 10 positions.
* The own blunders need d5-d6. The browser gets d5 in 1-3 of 10 and d6 in none.

The positions are slow because they are wide: the nodes per completed depth explode in cast-heavy
positions. They are not slow because the node rate is uniformly low.

## 3. Implications for the other round-3 workers

* **Generator coverage.** The policy stream (v27) does not generate 7 of the 10 MATE blows within
  500 candidates: Starfall, Corrupt, Scatter, Fury, Syzygy dash+cast, and the two "unknown" replies.
  The other three rank 9, 17 and 44. Depth 5-6 still finds four of the seven through other lines, but
  the browser does not reach depth 5-6 there.
  * Coverage is the cheaper lever: a blow ranked in the first 24 is searched at depth 4.
  * Gate any generator change with `--guest-only` and report the MATE column at d4/d5 and the
    coverage line.
* **Policy retrain.**
  * Weight the casts and dash+casts with non-default sacrifice pairs, in competitive positions.
  * The retrained policy must keep or raise policy-stream coverage of the 32 replies (17 / 19 / 21 / 21).
  * It must also raise the MATE count at depth 4 (1 of 10 today). That is what the browser actually
    runs at in those positions.
  * It must not lose v27's d5 cases (00:30 t23, 02:03 t16, 02:10 t23, 02:15 t18, 00:04 t34).
* **Search / node rate.**
  * Doubling the speed barely moves the decisive positions, because the tree widens: about +0.5 ply,
    and d6 stays at 10%.
  * So wasm threads (option B) or a node-rate pass alone will not deliver d6 at the blows. Narrower,
    better-ordered roots in cast-heavy positions will.
  * Measure node-rate work on competitive positions (`positions_rust_games.txt` or the guest
    positions), not `positions_midgame.txt` (0/76 competitive).
* **v27 vs v25 at depth 6.** v25 sees two MATE blows at d6 that v27 does not:
  * 05:46 t25 Corrupt.
  * 04:45 t31 Syzygy, the case the audit called "blind to d6" for v27.

  Whoever changes the eval or policy next should look at why (generator or eval).
* **Arena.** Any SPRT used for a release must include a `SIGIL_VARIANT=competitive` arm. The default
  is standard.
